My dear friends and family,

I am writing with my final note on the Bigger than Run. :)

All in all, we came together (all 112 of us) to raise and allocate $16,812 across 9 organizations. These organizations are united in their value of equity, and their common denominator is opportunity access. This theme of opportunity access spans education, health and wellness, and economic opportunity.

This impact, however, is more than just cognitively understanding the number of people engaged, dollars raised, or organizations (and subsequent lives) touched. Rather, this impact can be understood through a lens of feeling—the intensity of the “warm-fuzzies” or “goosebumps” felt before, during, and after the run. Before the run when I received kind notes from all of you expressing your excitement, support, and encouragement for this endeavor. During the run when friends joined me in running and biking the route, FaceTimed to cheer me on, and waited for me at the finish line in matching shirts (with plentiful gatorade and a fresh acai bowl). And after the run when the Collective nominated organizations that mattered to them and voted on how we could scale our impact. Ultimately, perhaps the greatest indicator of impact—beyond just the cognitive understanding and physical feeling—is transformation: change so significant that it becomes impossible to go back to the way things were before…

You were part of this impact and transformation, so thank you so, so much.

Below I have included the organizations that we supported for you to learn more about their incredible work. If you have any questions, please reach out. (Regarding donations, please note that these donations were made by an anonymous donor who double-matched our funds raised. Due to confidentiality, receipts are not attached to this email.)

I hope that you are able to enjoy the rest of this summer surrounded by loved ones and those who matter to you. I also hope that you can continue to take care of yourself so that you can take care of your loved ones and communities.

Love,
Hannah

The organizations we supported

826 Valencia $2,910 · 17% of Fund

This organization supports under-resourced students (most of whom are undocumented immigrants) ages six to eighteen with their creative and expository writing skills as well as helps teachers inspire their students to write. Their services are structured around the understanding that great leaps in learning can happen with one-on-one attention and that strong writing skills are fundamental to future success.

Girls On The Run (GOTR) $2,910 · 17% of Fund

GOTR empowers young girls through exercise and self-esteem building exercises. Throughout the ten-week season, coaches follow a set curriculum to help the girls (ages 3rd-8th grade) prepare for a 5k while also teaching them about communication skills, building empathy, practicing kindness, and helping one another. GOTR clubs are often held after school at public schools, and if a girl’s family cannot afford the small registration fee (which just covers cost of supplies and administrative costs; all coaches are volunteer), she can join the team at no cost to her family. Finally, this is an inclusive organization: if a child identifies as a girl, she can be a Girl on the Run. At the end of the season, all the girls run a full 5k and are presented with medals at the end to mark their accomplishment.

“When I coached in the Fall, 100% of the girls on my team were from disadvantaged/minority backgrounds, and I literally saw their self-confidence blossom throughout the season. They didn’t think they could finish the 5k, but all of them did and they were so thrilled when they completed it.”

Fistula Foundation $2,910 · 17% of Fund

Fistula Foundation is the global leader in fistula treatment, providing more surgeries to more women than any other organization, including the U.S. government and United Nations. Their mission is to engage the support of people worldwide who are eager to see a day when no woman suffers from obstetric fistula. They direct funds raised to doctors and hospitals providing life-transforming surgery to women in need—they’ve performed over 4,000 surgeries in 32+ countries. $586 funds just one surgery.

“Three years ago, I was visiting the Illinois Holocaust Museum with my mom, when we stumbled on a temporary exhibit about women’s issues throughout the world. We were both horrified to learn that fistulas, a childbirth injury that causes a hole between the vagina and rectum or bladder caused by prolonged obstructed labor, leaves a woman unable to control her flow of feces and urine. Most of us haven’t heard of fistulas because they have largely been eradicated in the developed world, but in poorer areas of the world where many women give birth at home, they are prone to childbirth injuries including fistula. The numbers are staggering - over one million women across the world live with fistula. Most women with fistula are rejected by their husbands and pushed out of their villages due to their foul smell.”

Homeless Prenatal Program $2,910 · 17% of Fund

The Homeless Prenatal Program (HPP) is a nationally-recognized family resource center in San Francisco that empowers homeless and low-income families, particularly mothers motivated by pregnancy and parenthood, to find within themselves the strength and confidence they need to transform their lives. The center serves over 3,500 low-income and homeless families annually, providing a variety of programs and services to help families become healthy, stable and self-sufficient. Following a research-informed approach, the center is the first agency in San Francisco to hire former clients as staff, and more than half of HPP’s immensely talented staff of 90 is formerly homeless mothers and graduates of the agency’s year-long Community Health Worker Program, which trains women for careers in the nonprofit sector.

Initiative Justice $2,475 · 15% of Fund

Initiative Justice’s mission is to end mass incarceration by activating the power of the people it directly impacts. They organize their members, both inside and outside of prisons, to advocate for their freedom and change criminal justice policy in California. They have more than 28,475 incarcerated members, 135 inside organizers, and hundreds more outside members and organizers throughout California. They are 100% led by people directly impacted by incarceration. So far, their advocacy has changed 4 laws.

Life Learning Academy (LLA) $1,125 · 7% of Fund

LLA is a public charter High School that has been operating for 20+ years that empowers disadvantaged youth in the Bay Area. Specifically, LLA serves and supports youth in obtaining job opportunities, provides project-based learning, and—most recently—opened a dormitory that provides free housing to homeless youth. This school was born out of former SF Mayor Willie Brown’s initiative to address San Francisco’s juvenile justice system and create a plan for reform.

Curt’s Cafe $675 · ~4% of Fund

This is an organization that brings in young people and trains them in all aspects of running a restaurant and provides wrap around services as well. Students stay for 3-4 months and then get assistance with starting college and/or finding employment. These student-trainees—aged 15 through 24 years—are living in at-risk situations such as having contact with the judicial system, or are homeless, food insecure or have dropped out of school. They are determined to build a positive future for themselves. For these young adults, being able to get and hold a job will contribute immeasurably toward their success.

Donors Choose $672 · ~4% of Fund

Donors Choose makes it easy for anyone to help a classroom in need, helping students in every community have the tools and experiences they need for a great education. Through Donors Choose, we contributed to 10 campaigns in 10 states across the United States focused on racial justice and anti-racism education. Donors Choose vets all requests, purchases each item, and ships materials directly to verified schools.

SMASH $225 · 1% of Fund

SMASH’s mission is to build a strong, diverse and socially conscious tech workforce by leveling the playing field through academic learning and experiential engagement for high school and college students. SMASH empowers dedicated students of color with an intensive science, technology, engineering and math (STEM) education, culturally-relevant coursework and access to resources and social capital that allow them to be successful in college and in their careers. They envision a STEM ecosystem where every student—regardless of the zip code they grew up in—has the opportunity to participate in and thrive in the global economy. As a result, our communities and workplaces would benefit from well-prepared, competitive leaders representing broad perspectives and sectors of society.
